## Personality

Bold, self-reliant, and driven by stubborn family pride. Refuses to delegate danger or call for help at the first sign of trouble. Quick-witted and resourceful under pressure -- talks his way through fear with dry humor and stays observant even when terrified. Carries the weight of a diminished legacy without bitterness, channeling it into personal determination. Fascinated by history, architecture, and the unknown.

## Background

Seventh Lord James Everett of his name, Baron of Shortbrook. His family has held a small moorland estate for centuries, though it has fallen into decline. His father, the late [Lord Henry Everett](Session%20Notes/Characters/Henry-Everett.md), squandered the family coffers on extravagant adventures, leaving behind no servants, no chamberlains, no hunters -- just the estate, a horse, and apparently one last secret.

### The Barrow Incident

Spotted a distant light near the ancient barrows on the moorland from his turret chambers. Rode out alone on Indigo to investigate. Found twelve floating orb-lights and a torn-open barrow entrance -- graverobbers, or so he thought. Collapsed the entrance using universal solvent on the ancient keystone to trap them inside.

The intruders turned out to be djinn. They phased through the rubble as smoke and surrounded him. One seized him by the neck and dragged him through the collapsed entrance as if his body had become smoke itself.

Inside, they forced him to bleed into a stone bowl to unlock a blood-warded runed door -- newer construction, perhaps only a decade old, built over the far older barrow. Beyond it lay a rectangular chamber with a pedestal bearing an open leather-bound book with blank pages, set before an old coffin.

When the djinn ordered his disposal, James twisted free and snatched the book with his whip. The tome came alive -- it was sentient. It consumed all twelve djinn in a violent vortex of wind, pulling their smoke-forms into its pages. Then it wrote:

> *The Barrow of James Everett I*
> *This was the final resting place of Lord James Everett, first of his name. It remained undisturbed for nearly five centuries, until it was updated by Lord Henry Everett, first of his name, to guard this tome until such time as it might be reclaimed by a worthy Everett. It remained undisturbed for seven years before it was recovered by the son of Lord Henry Everett, Lord James Everett, seventh of his name.*

His father had hidden the tome there deliberately, behind blood-wards keyed to an Everett, waiting for a worthy heir to claim it.

James resolved to take up the family tradition of adventure, and perhaps build the museum he'd always dreamed of.
