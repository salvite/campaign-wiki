# The Tome

**Category:** Magic / Artifact
**Related:** [James Everett](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Characters/James-Everett.md), [Henry Everett](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Characters/Henry-Everett.md), [Shortbrook Estate](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Locations/Shortbrook-Estate.md)

## Summary

A sentient leather-bound book recovered by [Lord James Everett](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Characters/James-Everett.md) from the Barrow of James Everett I on the [Shortbrook Estate](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Locations/Shortbrook-Estate.md). Hidden there by his father, [Lord Henry Everett](Session%20Notes/session%201/Vaeloris/Characters/Henry-Everett.md), behind blood-wards keyed to Everett lineage. The Tome can consume beings and inscribe knowledge onto its pages.

## Known Properties

- **Sentient:** Communicates by writing text on its own pages
- **Consumption:** Absorbed twelve djinn in a violent vortex of wind, pulling their smoke-forms into the pages. Referred to this as eating a "delicious snack" of "ancient knowledge"
- **Hunger:** Its first message stated it had "become quite hungry over the years"
- **Knowledge inscription:** After consuming the djinn, it wrote a detailed historical account of the barrow, demonstrating awareness of the Everett lineage and the barrow's full history
- **Pages appear blank** until activated -- text appeared only after James touched/held the book
- **Selective:** The area immediately around James was calm while the rest of the room was torn by a violent gale -- the Tome may protect its holder

## Known Text

First message (appeared when James held the book):
> *Well done, James. I have become quite hungry over the years, so it is thoughtful of you to bring me such a delicious snack. So much ancient knowledge.*

Second inscription (after consuming the djinn):
> *The Barrow of James Everett I*
> *This was the final resting place of Lord James Everett, first of his name. It remained undisturbed for nearly five centuries, until it was updated by Lord Henry Everett, first of his name, to guard this tome until such time as it might be reclaimed by a worthy Everett. It remained undisturbed for seven years before it was recovered by the son of Lord Henry Everett, Lord James Everett, seventh of his name.*
